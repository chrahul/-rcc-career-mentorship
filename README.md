
# The Technical Career Mentorship Program

**60 days. One to one. For engineers who know technology but do not know where they are going.**

Rahul Chaubey Consulting

---

## The promise

Most career advice is written by people who have never hired an engineer.

They can fix your resume. They cannot tell you whether you are actually ready for the role on it.

This program is built on the opposite premise. I have spent two decades in cloud, infrastructure and AI engineering, and I have sat on the hiring side for the exact roles you are targeting. So the first thing we do is not polish how you present yourself. The first thing we do is find out, honestly, where you stand against the bar for the role you want.

Then we close that distance, and only then do we package you.

**By day 60 you will know exactly which role you are going after, exactly what is missing, and you will have real evidence that you have closed it.**

---

## Who this is for

You are a fit if:

- You have real technical experience. Cloud, infrastructure, DevOps, SRE, platform or development
- You are somewhere between four and fifteen years in
- You are competent at your current job, and stuck
- You are targeting something higher. Solution Architect, Cloud Architect, Platform Lead, Engineering Manager, Principal Engineer, Head of Engineering
- You suspect there is a gap between where you are and where you want to be, but you cannot name it
- You are willing to do work between sessions

## Who this is not for

Be honest with yourself here. It saves us both time.

- You want someone to write your resume and nothing else
- You want to be taught a technology. This program does not teach Kubernetes, Terraform or AWS
- You are looking for a job referral service
- You are not prepared to hear an honest assessment of your current level
- You cannot commit four to six hours a week outside of sessions

## Stage by stage

### Intake. Before we meet

You complete the Career Dossier before session one. Full history, projects, technologies, scope of ownership, team size, what you actually built versus what you supported, compensation, constraints, and what you think you want.

This is not a form. It is the first piece of work, and how you complete it tells me a great deal.

**Output:** Career Dossier

---

### Stage 1. Technical Calibration
*Days 1 to 14*

This is the stage no career coach can run, and it is why this program exists.

I assess your real technical depth through structured conversation, the same way I would assess a candidate. Not certifications. Not what is on your resume. What you can actually explain, defend and troubleshoot.

We cover:

- Depth versus breadth in your current stack
- Design and architecture reasoning, not tool knowledge
- Production experience. What you have run, broken and fixed
- Scope of ownership. What was genuinely yours
- Where you are strong, and where you have been coasting
- How you communicate technical decisions

Then I tell you honestly where you sit against the bar for the role you think you want. Some people are closer than they believe. Some are further. Both answers are useful, and both are better than guessing.

**Output:** Calibration Scorecard. A written, specific assessment of your current technical level, your strengths, and your real weaknesses.

---

### Stage 2. Role Reality and Target Lock
*Days 15 to 28*

Most people target a job title they have never seen performed.

We fix that. For each role you are considering, we establish what the work actually is, who does it well, what a day looks like, what gets you promoted and what gets you quietly sidelined.

We cover:

- What the target role actually does, in reality rather than in job descriptions
- How to read a job description as market intelligence
- Which transitions from your current position are realistic, and which are not
- Where the role sits in different company types. Product, services, consulting, startup
- Market demand and compensation benchmarks for that role, at your experience level
- The difference between the next title and the next level

By the end of this stage you commit to one target. Not three. The entire rest of the program is built against it.

**Output:** Target Role Definition. One role, one level, one company profile, with evidence for why it is the right call.

---

### Stage 3. Gap Diagnosis and Evidence Plan
*Days 22 to 35*

This is where the program gets uncomfortable, and where it pays.

We put the Calibration Scorecard next to the Target Role Definition and name the distance between them precisely. Not "improve architecture skills". Something a person could actually act on this week.

We cover:

- The technical gap, named specifically
- The scope gap. Size of system, size of team, size of decision you have owned
- The visibility gap. Whether anyone outside your team knows what you can do
- The narrative gap. Whether your experience adds up to a story that points at this role
- Which gaps actually block the hire, and which are noise
- What evidence would close each one

Then we build the Evidence Plan. This is the heart of the program. For each blocking gap, we define the specific proof you will produce in the next three to four weeks. A designed and deployed system. A documented architecture. A written technical article. A talk. An internal initiative you lead.

I will point you at exactly what to study and build. I will not teach it to you in session. That is deliberate, and it is how this stays a mentorship rather than a course.

**Output:** Gap Diagnosis and Evidence Plan

---

### Stage 4. Evidence Build and Positioning
*Days 36 to 49*

You execute the Evidence Plan. I review the work and push back on it.

Only once there is evidence do we touch presentation. This is the inversion that makes this program different. Packaging a candidate before the substance exists just produces a convincing application that fails at the technical round.

We cover:

- Building and documenting the evidence, reviewed in session
- Architecture diagrams and technical writing that a hiring manager will respect
- Resume rebuilt around scope, decisions and impact, not responsibilities
- LinkedIn rebuilt for the target role, not your current one
- Your career narrative. Why this path, why this move, why now
- Positioning against the other candidates who will be in the same process
- Building visibility in public, so opportunities start arriving

**Outputs:** Evidence Ledger, Career Brand Kit. Resume, LinkedIn, narrative, bio, outreach messages.

---

### Stage 5. Conversion
*Days 50 to 60*

Now we turn all of it into an offer.

We cover:

- Mock interviews where I play the hiring manager for your target role and level. Technical depth, architecture, behavioural, leadership
- Your story bank. Every significant project converted into an answer that lands
- The questions that actually decide senior technical hires
- How to handle the gap you have not fully closed yet, honestly
- Search strategy. Target companies, referrals, recruiters, applications, tracking
- Compensation benchmarking and negotiation for your level
- Evaluating and comparing offers
- Your first 90 days in the new role, so the move actually sticks

**Outputs:** Interview Story Bank, Search Plan, Offer and Negotiation Strategy, First 90 Days Plan

## What this program is not

**It is not technical training.** I will tell you precisely what to learn and build. I will review what you produce. I will not teach you the technology in session. There are cheaper and better ways to learn Kubernetes than paying for one to one time.

**It is not a resume service.** The resume is produced in Stage 4, after there is something worth putting on it.

**It is not a job guarantee.** I can close the distance between you and the role. I cannot control the market, the timing, or the interview on the day.

**It is not comfortable.** Stage 1 and Stage 3 involve hearing accurate things about your current level. Everyone says they want honest feedback. Fewer people enjoy receiving it.
